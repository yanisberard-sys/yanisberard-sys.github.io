Three TIPE projects

2022 – 2023 · Lycée La Martinière Monplaisir

Elections: the rules of the democratic game

Which voting system should a society use? Arrow lists a few natural axioms and shows that, with three candidates or more, no system satisfies all of them. The project proves the theorem with decisive coalitions, then moves to computational social choice: plurality, Borda and Condorcet winners, and how often they disagree when ballots are drawn at random.

2023 – 2024 · Lycée La Martinière Monplaisir

Heads or tails: paradoxes and random walks

Alice and Bob toss a coin and each bets on a pattern. Computing mean waiting times explains why some bets win more often than intuition says. The project then turns to random walks on Zd and Pólya's theorem: the simple random walk comes back to its starting point almost surely if and only if d≤2.

2024 – 2025 · Lycée du Parc

Lattices of finite groups and a game

Two players, Abel and Burnside, take turns putting elements of a finite group into a bag. Whoever makes the bag generate the whole group loses. The subgroup lattice and the Frattini subgroup decide who has a winning strategy: the project settles cyclic groups, groups such as GLn​(Fp​), and finite abelian groups.
