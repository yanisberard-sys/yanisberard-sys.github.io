Complex algebraic geometry

Local holomorphy and global algebraicity: the road to GAGA

Institut Camille Jordan, LyonMay – June 2026Supervised by Jean-Yves Welschinger

My first research internship followed a single thread through the theory of compact Riemann surfaces: how an object defined by local, analytic data turns out to be globally algebraic.

The report starts from covering theory and the Riemann–Hurwitz formula, builds sheaf cohomology, and proves the Riemann–Roch theorem and Serre duality. These give enough meromorphic functions to embed every compact Riemann surface in projective space.

Chow's theorem then shows that closed analytic subsets of projective space are algebraic. GAGA for curves completes the picture: on a projective curve, analytic and algebraic coherent sheaves have the same cohomology.

No simple groups below 60

The only simple groups of order less than 60 are the cyclic groups of prime order. The bound is sharp: the rotation group of the icosahedron is A₅, of order 60.

Three TIPE projects

2022 – 2023 · Lycée La Martinière Monplaisir

Elections: the rules of the democratic game

Which voting system should a society use? Arrow lists a few natural axioms and shows that, with three candidates or more, no system satisfies all of them. The project proves the theorem with decisive coalitions, then moves to computational social choice: plurality, Borda and Condorcet winners, and how often they disagree when ballots are drawn at random.

See the slides →
2023 – 2024 · Lycée La Martinière Monplaisir

Heads or tails: paradoxes and random walks

Alice and Bob toss a coin and each bets on a pattern. Computing mean waiting times explains why some bets win more often than intuition says. The project then turns to random walks on ℤᵈ and Pólya's theorem: the simple random walk comes back to its starting point almost surely if and only if d ≤ 2.

See the slides →
2024 – 2025 · Lycée du Parc

Lattices of finite groups and a game

Two players, Abel and Burnside, take turns putting elements of a finite group into a bag. Whoever makes the bag generate the whole group loses. The subgroup lattice and the Frattini subgroup decide who has a winning strategy: the project settles cyclic groups, groups such as GLₙ(𝔽ₚ), and finite abelian groups.
